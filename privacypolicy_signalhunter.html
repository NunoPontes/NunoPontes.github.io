Signal Hunter – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026

Signal Hunter (“the app”) shows the Bluetooth devices and WiFi networks near you, helps you find one of them by its signal strength, maps the WiFi signal in your home and helps you check a room. This policy explains what information the app uses, why, and the choices you have. The app is provided by Nuno Pontes (“I”, “me”).

In short

  • There are no accounts. I do not collect your name, email, contacts, photos or location, or anything you type into the app.
  • The app only listens to radio signals. It never connects to a nearby device. The names, addresses and signal readings of nearby devices and networks stay on your phone.
  • The app uses Google services for ads, anonymous analytics and crash reports. Ads and analytics follow your consent choice (asked first where the law requires it), which you can change at any time in Settings → Ad privacy choices. Crash reports are sent when the app crashes.
  • Removing the ads is a one-time purchase handled by Google Play. I never see your payment details.

Information that stays on your device

While a scan runs, the app reads the names, hardware addresses and signal strengths of nearby Bluetooth devices and WiFi networks, and the name of the WiFi network you are connected to. It uses them only to show them on the screen. They are not sent to me or to anyone else. Scans run only while the app is open on the screen.

The WiFi signal map saves the place names you type and their signal readings in the app's private storage. Your settings (theme, language, the network scan switch and similar choices) are stored there too. Room check results are kept only while the app is open. Removing the app, or clearing its data in Android settings, deletes all of this. If you turn on Android's backup, Google may store a copy of the saved data in your Google account under your own Google settings.

When you choose to share a signal map (CSV) or a room check report (Markdown), the app hands the file to the app you pick in Android's share menu. Where it goes afterwards is your choice.

The network scan

The network scan is off until you turn it on in Settings and confirm that you own the network or have permission to scan it. It looks only at devices on your own private local network: it sends ping and connection checks to local addresses and listens to the local device announcements (mDNS and SSDP). It never connects to devices on the internet, never logs in to a device, and never sends the results anywhere.

Permissions

  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth scan and connect): to list nearby Bluetooth devices and their signal strength, and to show the names of devices already paired with your phone. The app never connects to them.
  • Nearby WiFi devices, WiFi state: to list nearby WiFi networks and read the signal of the network you are connected to.
  • Location: Android needs this permission for Bluetooth and WiFi scans on older versions, and some versions also need the location setting to be on. The app never reads your GPS position or any other location.
  • WiFi multicast: only for the network scan, to hear the local device announcements.
  • Vibration and the torch: for the hunt feedback and the manual room sweep. The torch uses the camera flash only as a light. The app never takes photos or video.
  • Internet and network state: used for ads, analytics, crash reports, update checks, purchases and the local network scan.
  • Advertising ID: used by the ads service, only according to your consent and your device's advertising settings.

The app does not use your camera for pictures, your microphone or your contacts.

Consent and your choices

  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and other regions that require it, the app shows Google's consent form on first launch. Analytics and ads are only used according to your answer. You can open the form again from Settings → Ad privacy choices.
  • You can reset or delete your Android advertising ID, or opt out of ad personalisation, in your device's Google settings (Settings → Google → Ads).
  • You can turn off the Bluetooth, WiFi and location permissions at any time in Android settings. The scans then stop working, and the rest of the app keeps working.
  • Removing ads stops the ads.
  • Debug builds used for development never send analytics or crash data.

Retention

Analytics and crash data are kept by Google for the periods configured in Firebase (analytics event data is kept for up to 14 months). I do not keep any other copy of personal data.

Responsible use

Use Signal Hunter only to find your own devices, or devices and networks you have permission to look for. A clean room check never proves that a room has no camera or other device.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age in your country) and I do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Security

Data sent to Google services travels over encrypted connections. Locally stored data is kept in the app's private storage.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR or UK GDPR) you may have the right to access, correct or delete your data, to object to or restrict processing, and to withdraw consent. Because I cannot identify you from the anonymous data the app collects, the easiest way to exercise these rights is to withdraw consent in the app and reset your advertising ID. You can also contact me, and you may complain to your local data protection authority.
